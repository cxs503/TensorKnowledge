# Vision

## Mission

Build an open knowledge and learning framework for the AI era that helps every person develop the understanding, judgment, imagination, and collaborative capability needed to explore and create.

## The transition

Industrial-era education was shaped by scarce expert labor, specialized occupations, and the transfer of established knowledge. AI changes the cost and speed of information retrieval, analysis, execution, and coordination. It does **not** eliminate the need for human understanding, evidence, judgment, responsibility, or purpose.

The educational response should be neither "learn nothing because AI knows it" nor "memorize everything as before." It should combine durable foundations with the ability to mobilize external knowledge and agent capabilities.

## Human capabilities to cultivate

- **Understand:** build mental models, reason quantitatively, interpret language and representations.
- **Investigate:** observe, measure, ask questions, formulate hypotheses, and design tests.
- **Judge:** assess evidence, uncertainty, assumptions, validity, bias, and consequences.
- **Connect:** transfer ideas across disciplines and connect text, mathematics, code, data, images, simulations, and experiments.
- **Collaborate:** work effectively with people and agents while retaining agency and accountability.
- **Create:** produce original explanations, theories, designs, works, and institutions.
- **Orient:** deliberate about values, purpose, fairness, responsibility, and long-term futures.

## Human capability framework

The cross-cutting capability architecture is specified in [`curriculum/human-capabilities-ai-era.md`](curriculum/human-capabilities-ai-era.md), with a machine-readable summary in [`curriculum/human-capabilities-framework.json`](curriculum/human-capabilities-framework.json). It guides curriculum and assessment design; it is a draft to be reviewed, not a claim that these capabilities are uniquely human or permanently beyond AI.

## Human–agent relationship

TensorKnowledge adopts a human-agency-first approach. Agents can extend memory, search, analysis, simulation, and execution. Humans remain responsible for goals, interpretation, consequential decisions, and the ethical use of outcomes.

The project is open to exploring increasingly autonomous agents, but does not assume that agent autonomy automatically confers moral status, rights, or reliability. Those questions require careful philosophical, legal, and empirical work.

## Educational principles

1. Strong foundations remain essential.
2. Learning should be problem-centered and inquiry-driven.
3. Claims must be distinguishable from evidence and uncertainty.
4. Multiple representations should illuminate the same underlying phenomenon.
5. Agent assistance must be transparent and critically evaluated.
6. Assessment should test understanding, transfer, judgment, and creation—not merely polished output.
7. Knowledge should be revisable, traceable, and open to plural perspectives.
8. Accessibility, inclusion, learner autonomy, and privacy are design requirements.

## Long-term aspiration

A person should be able to begin with a meaningful question, identify the knowledge needed, collaborate with suitable agents and other people, conduct credible investigations, and turn the result into something useful, beautiful, explanatory, or socially valuable.

The ambition is not to make every person an expert in every field. It is to expand each person's ability to enter unfamiliar fields, learn responsibly, and participate in creation.
