# Curriculum Map

The curriculum is organized into six interconnected domains. Each domain should contain learning pathways, concept records, tutorials, practice, and assessment evidence.

## 01 — Cognitive Foundations

Language, reading, writing, and argument; mathematics and quantitative reasoning; logic, probability, and decision reasoning; computation and algorithmic thinking; information and media literacy; visual, spatial, and multimodal literacy.

## 02 — Evidence and Reality

Observation, measurement, and operational definitions; data quality, sampling, and uncertainty; experimental design and reproducibility; statistics, inference, and causal reasoning; source evaluation and scientific integrity; risk, evidence, and decision-making.

## 03 — Models and Principles

Mathematical models and abstraction; physical principles and mechanisms; living systems and evolution; mind, behavior, and cognition; economic mechanisms and incentives; social, institutional, and computational models.

## 04 — Systems and Interdisciplinary Thinking

Systems thinking, feedback, and control; complexity, emergence, and networks; energy, material, information, and resource flows; ecology, infrastructure, and coupled systems; interdisciplinary research and knowledge transfer; multiscale and lifecycle reasoning.

## 05 — Creativity and Discovery

Question formulation and hypothesis generation; scientific research and engineering design; prototyping, iteration, and evaluation; art, narrative, media, and design; human–agent collaboration; entrepreneurship and social innovation.

## 06 — Civilization and Values

Philosophy and theories of knowledge; ethics, responsibility, and governance; history, culture, and institutions; economics, distribution, and public goods; technology, society, and future studies; human flourishing and environmental stewardship.

## Pathway design

A pathway should begin with a learner goal or real question, specify entry requirements, link relevant concepts across domains, and culminate in an observable artifact, explanation, or investigation. Avoid treating this outline as a fixed universal sequence.
