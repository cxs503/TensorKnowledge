# Capability Progression Rubric

**Status:** Draft, version 0.1.0. Use as a formative evidence guide, not a universal scale, credential, or measure of personal worth.

## Principles

- Judge a specific capability in a stated context, not the person.
- Record evidence and conditions, including tools, scaffolds, time, and collaboration.
- A learner may show different levels across contexts and dimensions.
- Do not infer a level from confidence, speed, polished presentation, or agent-generated output alone.
- Use accessible ways to demonstrate capability and allow reassessment.
- Levels are descriptive anchors, not mandatory sequential stages.

## Levels

| Level | Descriptor | Observable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| L0 — Not yet evidenced | There is not enough relevant evidence to make a judgment. | No suitable sample, or the task/context was inaccessible or unclear. This is not a claim of inability. |
| L1 — Supported orientation | The learner can participate with substantial guidance or scaffolding. | Identifies key terms or steps with prompts; follows a worked example; can point out where help is needed. |
| L2 — Independent in familiar contexts | The learner can explain and perform a bounded task in a familiar setting. | Gives a coherent explanation; performs the task with appropriate checks; recognizes common limitations. |
| L3 — Adaptive transfer | The learner adapts the capability to a new or less structured context. | Selects or modifies methods; explains changed assumptions; handles plausible alternatives and uncertainty. |
| L4 — Original contribution | The learner creates a defensible new artifact, question, synthesis, or method. | States what is novel; supports it with evidence or testing; responds constructively to critique; documents limitations. |
| L5 — Stewardship and enablement | The learner helps others use, critique, improve, or govern the capability responsibly. | Develops reusable guidance; mentors or convenes review; anticipates downstream effects; maintains correction and accountability mechanisms. |

## Evidence dimensions

For each assessment, record the relevant dimensions rather than collapsing everything into one number:

1. **Conceptual explanation** — accuracy, coherence, mechanisms, assumptions.
2. **Execution** — method selection, quality of performance, appropriate tool use.
3. **Evidence judgment** — source quality, uncertainty, alternative explanations, verification.
4. **Transfer** — adaptation to changed conditions and recognition of limits.
5. **Creation** — originality, usefulness, testability, and response to critique.
6. **Agency and responsibility** — decision ownership, transparent assistance, privacy, safety, and consequences.
7. **Communication and collaboration** — clarity, attribution, listening, and constructive review.

## Recording a judgment

A useful record includes:
- capability and context;
- task and success criteria;
- evidence artifacts and dates;
- assistance and collaboration conditions;
- dimension-specific observations;
- current descriptor with rationale;
- uncertainty, accessibility considerations, and next practice opportunity.

Prefer statements such as “L2 evidence for causal reasoning in the pendulum investigation, with prompts to distinguish correlation from intervention” over “the learner is Level 2.”

## Agent-assisted evidence

When an agent contributes, distinguish:
- learner-authored decisions and reasoning;
- agent-generated or agent-executed material;
- learner checks, edits, and independent reproduction;
- unresolved or unverified contributions.

An agent's performance is not the learner's evidence. For important capabilities, include a proportionate independent explanation or performance sample.

## Safeguards

Do not use this draft rubric alone for admissions, employment, high-stakes placement, diagnosis, or consequential judgments about an individual. Such uses require validated instruments, due process, qualified oversight, and context-specific evidence. Avoid cross-cultural or cross-disciplinary comparisons without validation.
