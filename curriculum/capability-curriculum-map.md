# Capability-to-Curriculum Mapping

**Status:** draft, v0.1.0  
**Purpose:** make the human-capability framework actionable without creating a one-capability/one-subject curriculum.

## Mapping principles

- A capability is developed across multiple knowledge domains and contexts.
- A knowledge node may support several capabilities.
- A pathway should integrate knowledge, practice, judgment, and reflection.
- Assessments must collect evidence of the learner's own reasoning, including when agents assist.
- A mapping is a design hypothesis until reviewed and piloted.

## First-cycle capability map

| Priority capability | Relevant knowledge nodes | Learning pathway | Required learner evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame a meaningful question | observation, hypothesis, critical thinking, system thinking | Scientific inquiry; Systems and decisions | A scoped question, explicit assumptions, and rationale for why it matters |
| Find and evaluate evidence | observation, measurement, evidence, uncertainty, critical thinking | Scientific inquiry; Systems and decisions | Claim–evidence table, source appraisal, and calibrated confidence |
| Build and test a model | model, mathematical modeling, measurement, uncertainty | Scientific inquiry; Systems and decisions | Model with variables, assumptions, units, validation attempt, and limitations |
| Reason about causality | evidence, experimental design, causal reasoning, uncertainty | Scientific inquiry; Systems and decisions | Competing causal explanations and a defensible test or comparison |
| Work with an agent and verify its contribution | critical thinking, reproducibility, evidence, ethical reasoning | Both pathways, with explicit agent-assisted tasks | Task specification, provenance, independent checks, corrections, and explanation of what the learner understands |
| Communicate a justified conclusion | evidence, model, uncertainty, critical thinking | Both pathways | Clear explanation separating observations, inference, uncertainty, and value judgments |
| Create and account for consequences | creativity, system thinking, ethical reasoning | Systems and decisions; future design pathway | Original artifact or proposal, test evidence, stakeholder analysis, and trade-off reflection |

## Assessment architecture

For each priority capability, collect evidence in four complementary modes:

1. **Explain:** the learner explains the concept or decision without relying on a generated answer.
2. **Perform:** the learner carries out a method, analysis, or practical task.
3. **Transfer:** the learner adapts the method to a new case with changed conditions.
4. **Reflect and account:** the learner identifies uncertainty, limitations, agent contribution, and consequences.

A single multiple-choice test or polished final artifact is insufficient for high-confidence claims about capability.

## Agent-use disclosure

When agents contribute, the learning record should distinguish:

- what the learner decided and specified;
- what the agent generated or executed;
- what evidence was inspected;
- what the learner independently reproduced or verified;
- what was changed, rejected, or left uncertain.

Do not require learners to disclose irrelevant private conversation content. Record only information needed to establish learning evidence and provenance, with appropriate consent and data minimization.

## Pilot protocol

A pilot should recruit a small, clearly described learner group and record:
- prior knowledge and relevant access needs;
- the task and permitted agent assistance;
- time and resources available;
- learner artifacts and brief oral or written explanations;
- observed confusion, failure, and help-seeking;
- evaluator rubric and inter-rater disagreements;
- revisions made after the pilot.

Report sample size and context. Do not generalize from a small pilot to all learners.

## Review status

This mapping is an initial design hypothesis. Expert review, learner pilot, accessibility review, and assessment validity review remain pending.
