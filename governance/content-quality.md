# Content Quality and Review Governance

## Quality principle

A tutorial is a learning product, not just a text artifact. Quality requires correctness, clarity, useful practice, valid assessment, accessibility, and evidence that learners can achieve the stated outcomes.

## Review gates

### Gate 1 — Editorial completeness
Purpose, audience, prerequisites, and outcomes are clear. Structure follows the tutorial specification. Language is accessible and terms are defined.

### Gate 2 — Subject-matter accuracy
Important claims are supported by suitable sources. Equations, code, examples, units, and terminology are checked. Assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations are explicit. A qualified reviewer is identified for specialist content.

### Gate 3 — Practice and reproducibility
Exercises work as described. Data, code, and instructions are available and appropriately licensed. Expected results and common failure modes are documented. Learner tasks require active reasoning.

### Gate 4 — Learning effectiveness
Assessment maps to outcomes. A learner pilot or structured expert walkthrough is completed. Confusion points and failure patterns are recorded. Revisions respond to evidence, not only author preference.

### Gate 5 — Publication and maintenance
Sources, version, review status, and change history are visible. Accessibility and privacy have been considered. A maintenance owner or review trigger is identified.

## Agent production policy

Agents may draft, summarize sources, generate examples, translate, test code, and critique content. Agent output is untrusted until checked. Substantive educational claims and specialist material require human review appropriate to the subject. Automated checks complement, but do not replace, expert and learner review.

## Defect severity

- **Critical:** dangerous advice, major factual error, fabricated evidence, or materially misleading instruction. Withdraw until corrected.
- **Major:** incorrect core concept, broken central exercise, invalid assessment, or unsupported central claim. Blocks validation.
- **Minor:** localized ambiguity, formatting issue, or nonessential error. Correct promptly.

## Correction process

Record the affected version, issue, impact, correction, reviewer, and date. Preserve a transparent history. Do not silently rewrite material when the change affects learner understanding or a cited conclusion.
