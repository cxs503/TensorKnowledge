# Knowledge Evolution and Provenance

## Why evolution needs governance

Knowledge changes. New evidence may strengthen, narrow, challenge, or supersede an explanation. A responsible learning system must make revision possible without erasing the history of how conclusions were reached.

## Provenance requirements

For substantive claims, record author or originating institution; publication or collection date; version, DOI, URL, or persistent identifier; evidence type and scope; relevant methods, assumptions, and limitations; and access date when the source may change.

For datasets and generated artifacts, also record licenses, transformations, software, parameters, and responsible parties where feasible.

## Update workflow

1. Propose a change with rationale and affected assets.
2. Identify whether it is a correction, clarification, extension, or contested interpretation.
3. Review evidence quality and possible downstream effects.
4. Obtain appropriate subject-matter review.
5. Update affected tutorials and concept links.
6. Record version, date, reviewer, and summary.
7. Notify users when a change materially alters prior guidance.

## Disagreement

Preserve credible disagreement when evidence or values are contested. Explain points of agreement, sources of disagreement, and what evidence could change the assessment. Do not manufacture consensus.

## Agent role

Agents can monitor sources, identify stale references, compare versions, and propose updates. They cannot independently certify a claim as true. Human editors remain accountable for publication decisions.
