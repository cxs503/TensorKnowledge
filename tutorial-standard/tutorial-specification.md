# Tutorial Specification

This document defines the minimum structure for a TensorKnowledge tutorial. Adapt the format to the topic, but do not omit accuracy, learning outcomes, practice, or assessment.

## Required sections

1. **Title and purpose** — what the tutorial helps the learner understand or do.
2. **Audience and prerequisites** — who it is for and what is assumed.
3. **Driving question** — a meaningful question or phenomenon.
4. **Learning outcomes** — observable capabilities, using verbs such as explain, derive, evaluate, design, compare, or create.
5. **Concept map** — key concepts, dependencies, and links to other domains.
6. **Core explanation** — definitions, mechanisms, examples, assumptions, and limits.
7. **Multiple representations** — text, mathematics, diagrams, data, code, audio/video, or physical demonstrations where useful.
8. **Worked example** — a transparent example with reasoning, not only a final answer.
9. **Practice** — exercises requiring active learner work.
10. **Agent collaboration** — optional tasks for agent assistance, including verification and failure analysis.
11. **Open exploration** — a question the learner can investigate independently.
12. **Assessment** — evidence that outcomes have been achieved.
13. **Sources and provenance** — authoritative references, datasets, licenses, and attribution.
14. **Limitations and review record** — uncertainty, known errors, reviewer, date, and version.

## Learning progression

Where suitable, include three levels:

- **Foundation:** understand essential vocabulary and explain the central idea.
- **Depth:** work with mechanisms, formal representations, assumptions, and limitations.
- **Creation:** transfer the idea to a new context, formulate a question, or produce and validate an artifact.

These are depth options, not mandatory age or ability categories.

## Agent-assisted learning protocol

1. State the learner's objective.
2. Specify what the agent may do and what the learner must do.
3. Preserve prompts, relevant outputs, tools, and sources when reproducibility matters.
4. Require the learner to inspect assumptions and verify important claims.
5. Include at least one plausible agent failure mode.
6. Assess the learner's understanding independently of the agent's output.

Generated content is not evidence merely because an agent produced it.

## Writing style

Define technical terms; separate established knowledge from interpretation and speculation; prefer concrete examples; explain why methods work and when they fail; avoid false certainty; provide accessible alternatives.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Outcomes are observable and assessable.
- [ ] Prerequisites and audience are explicit.
- [ ] Claims have suitable sources.
- [ ] Assumptions and limitations are stated.
- [ ] Worked examples show reasoning.
- [ ] Learners actively practice.
- [ ] Agent use is transparent and critically evaluated.
- [ ] Assessment measures understanding and transfer.
- [ ] Accessibility and licensing are addressed.
- [ ] Review status and version are recorded.
