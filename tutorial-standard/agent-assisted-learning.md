# Agent-Assisted Learning Standard

## Principle

Agents should expand a learner's reach while strengthening—not replacing—the learner's own understanding, judgment, and agency.

## Appropriate uses

Explain concepts at different levels; suggest prerequisites and learning pathways; generate practice questions; inspect data and write code; compare explanations and assumptions; critique drafts or experimental designs; locate sources for subsequent verification.

## Learner responsibilities

The learner should be able to state the question and desired outcome; explain reasoning behind important conclusions; identify what the agent did and what the learner did; verify consequential claims; recognize uncertainty and limitations; defend or revise the final result.

## Common failure modes to teach

- fabricated or misrepresented sources;
- confident but incorrect explanations;
- hidden assumptions and omitted boundary conditions;
- code that runs but implements the wrong method;
- data leakage or invalid evaluation;
- plausible visualizations from incorrect data;
- overgeneralization beyond a model's validity;
- sycophancy and uncritical agreement.

## Assessment rule

Do not grade learners solely on the polish or complexity of an agent-produced artifact. Include explanation, transfer to a new problem, independent checking, and reflection on errors.

## Privacy and safety

Do not ask learners to disclose sensitive personal information to agents. Use approved data, respect licenses, and follow relevant institutional and legal requirements. High-impact decisions require appropriate expert oversight.
