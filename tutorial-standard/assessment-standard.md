# Learning Assessment Standard

## What assessment should establish

Assessment should provide evidence that a learner can understand, judge, transfer, and create—not merely reproduce text or submit an agent-generated artifact.

## Four dimensions

### 1. Understanding
Can the learner explain concepts and mechanisms in their own words, interpret formal representations, and state assumptions?

### 2. Judgment
Can the learner evaluate evidence, uncertainty, sources, model validity, and agent outputs? Can they identify errors and explain why they matter?

### 3. Transfer
Can the learner apply an idea to an unfamiliar example, identify where an analogy breaks, and adapt a method to a new context?

### 4. Creation
Can the learner formulate a worthwhile question, develop alternatives, produce an artifact or investigation, test it, and explain what has been learned?

## Evidence methods

Use a suitable combination of concept explanations and diagrams; derivations or worked reasoning; critique of a flawed claim or agent response; practical demonstrations and experiments; unfamiliar transfer problems; project artifacts and design records; reflective accounts of decisions and revisions.

## Rubric guidance

- **Beginning:** recognizes terms but cannot yet explain relationships.
- **Developing:** explains core ideas with support; misses some assumptions.
- **Competent:** explains and applies ideas correctly in familiar contexts.
- **Advanced:** transfers ideas, evaluates limitations, and handles ambiguity.
- **Generative:** formulates new questions or approaches and validates the result.

Do not collapse all dimensions into one score unless justified. Report strengths and gaps separately.

## Integrity

Disclose permitted agent assistance. Where independent capability matters, include a task completed without agent-generated reasoning or require the learner to defend the work in detail. Reward honest uncertainty and correction, not confident presentation.
