# Knowledge Taxonomy

This taxonomy is a practical indexing scheme for learning assets. It should remain lightweight and evolve through use.

## Knowledge object types

- **Concept:** a defined idea or category.
- **Fact / observation:** a claim about an observed or recorded state, with source and context.
- **Principle / law:** a general relationship, with stated scope and limitations.
- **Model / theory:** a structured explanation or predictive representation.
- **Method:** a repeatable procedure for inquiry or action.
- **Skill:** an ability demonstrated through performance.
- **Dataset:** structured observations with provenance, units, and collection conditions.
- **Experiment:** a designed intervention or observation protocol.
- **Case study:** a contextualized account of a real or constructed situation.
- **Artifact:** a produced result such as code, design, essay, simulation, or artwork.
- **Question / hypothesis:** an unresolved issue or testable proposed explanation.
- **Value / norm:** a stated evaluative commitment, with context and possible conflicts.

## Relationships

Use explicit relationships where useful: prerequisite-for, part-of, instance-of, explains, predicts, supported-by, challenges, measured-by, represented-by, implemented-by, applies-to, derived-from, analogous-to, conflicts-with, extends, supersedes.

Relationships do not imply certainty by themselves. An analogy is not proof of equivalence.

## Metadata recommended for each learning asset

Stable identifier; title and summary; language; domain and topic tags; intended audience and prerequisites; learning outcomes; knowledge object types; related concepts and assets; sources and provenance; author and reviewer roles; last reviewed date; review status; version and change history; accessibility notes; agent assistance disclosure.

## Evidence status vocabulary

- **Established:** strongly supported within a clearly stated scope.
- **Well-supported:** substantial evidence, with some remaining uncertainty.
- **Provisional:** plausible but not yet sufficiently tested.
- **Contested:** credible disagreement exists.
- **Speculative:** exploratory idea without adequate evidence.
- **Superseded:** retained for history but no longer recommended.

Do not use these labels as universal numerical confidence scores. Explain the basis and scope of the judgment.
