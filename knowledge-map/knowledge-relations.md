# Knowledge Relations and Multimodal Representation

## Core idea

A real phenomenon can be described through multiple representations. Text, equations, diagrams, code, measurements, images, videos, simulations, and physical experiments are not interchangeable, but can provide complementary views of the same object or process.

Example: flow past a cylinder may be represented by a verbal description of vortex shedding; governing equations and dimensionless parameters; measured force time series; velocity, pressure, and vorticity fields; simulation code and numerical settings; laboratory images or video.

A useful knowledge system links these representations while preserving their distinct meanings, assumptions, units, resolution, and provenance.

## Representation record

Record where possible:
- what object, process, or claim it refers to;
- representation type and format;
- source, author, instrument, or generating model;
- time, location, coordinate system, and units;
- assumptions and transformations;
- resolution, uncertainty, and known limitations;
- links to related representations and claims.

## Linking rules

1. Link only when the shared referent or relationship is clear.
2. Preserve original data and transformations; do not silently replace one with another.
3. Distinguish a visualization from the underlying measurement or simulation.
4. Distinguish model output from empirical observation.
5. Record disagreements rather than forcing false consistency.
6. Make it possible to trace a conclusion back to evidence and methods.

## Learning design implication

Use multiple representations when they improve understanding, not merely to appear multimedia-rich. Ask learners to translate between representations: explain a graph in words, derive a relationship, inspect a field visualization, or compare a simulation with measurement.
