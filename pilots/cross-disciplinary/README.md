# Cross-Disciplinary Learning Pilot

**Status:** Design brief only — not yet run with learners.  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Purpose:** test whether the TensorKnowledge learning method can support meaningful learning and transfer across scientific inquiry, engineering design, humanities research, and creative production.

## 1. Why this pilot

A learning methodology should not be judged only by how well it explains itself. It must work across disciplines with different standards of evidence and different forms of legitimate output.

This pilot uses one shared learning cycle and four distinct disciplinary tasks. It tests both the common method and the limits of standardization. It is not intended to prove that all disciplines can be reduced to one assessment model.

## 2. Pilot questions

1. Can learners use the same orient–diagnose–map–understand–practice–verify–reflect–transfer–create–steward cycle in different fields?
2. Which parts of the cycle transfer, and which require discipline-specific adaptation?
3. Does agent assistance improve access, feedback, or exploration without displacing learner reasoning?
4. Can assessors distinguish learner understanding from polished agent-generated output?
5. Are the evidence requirements understandable, accessible, and proportionate?

## 3. Four pilot studios

### Studio A — Scientific inquiry: explain a measurable phenomenon

**Challenge:** investigate how the period of a pendulum changes with a selected variable, such as length, while keeping other relevant conditions controlled.

**Learner work**
- State a question and prediction before consulting an agent.
- Identify variables, measurement procedure, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Collect or analyze documented measurements.
- Compare observations with a simple model and discuss deviations.
- Explain what the evidence does and does not support.

**Evidence:** pre-investigation prediction, procedure, raw data, analysis, uncertainty statement, model comparison, and an agent-free explanation.

**Discipline-specific quality:** measurement quality, controlled comparison, model assumptions, uncertainty, and reproducibility.

**Safety/accessibility:** use a small, stable setup; avoid suspended heavy objects; offer a supplied dataset or accessible simulation when physical measurement is unsuitable. Clearly label supplied or simulated data.

### Studio B — Engineering design: build and test a constrained artifact

**Challenge:** design a paper bridge spanning a specified gap using a fixed quantity and type of paper and a defined joining method. The pilot facilitator sets the exact constraints before the activity.

**Learner work**
- Translate needs and constraints into measurable requirements.
- Sketch alternatives and explain material/geometry choices.
- Build a prototype, test it consistently, and record failure modes.
- Revise the design and compare results against the first version.
- Explain trade-offs and limits of the test.

**Evidence:** requirements, sketches, prototype versions, test protocol, measurements, failure observations, and design rationale.

**Discipline-specific quality:** requirements traceability, fair testing, iteration, functional performance, and honest reporting of limitations.

**Safety/accessibility:** use ordinary paper and low-risk joining materials; keep loads small and controlled; do not use sharp tools or hazardous weights. Provide a digital design or analysis alternative where needed.

### Studio C — Humanities research: interpret evidence in context

**Challenge:** investigate a bounded historical or cultural question using at least two attributable primary sources and relevant contextual scholarship. The facilitator and learner choose a topic appropriate to available sources and learner context.

**Learner work**
- Formulate a question without assuming that sources tell the same story.
- Identify authorship, date, audience, purpose, provenance, and missing perspectives.
- Compare claims and distinguish source statements from interpretation.
- Develop an evidence-backed interpretation while considering plausible alternatives.
- State whose perspectives are absent and what cannot be concluded.

**Evidence:** research question, source register, provenance/context notes, claim-evidence matrix, interpretation, counter-reading, and limitations statement.

**Discipline-specific quality:** contextual reading, source criticism, interpretive reasoning, treatment of disagreement, and transparent distinction between evidence and inference.

**Integrity:** do not fabricate quotations, citations, or provenance. Respect copyright and cultural restrictions. Sensitive histories require appropriate contextual and specialist review.

### Studio D — Creative production: make and critique an original work

**Challenge:** create a short explanatory or narrative media work for a named audience, using a topic selected by the learner. A suggested format is a 60–90 second storyboard-led video or an equivalent accessible medium.

**Learner work**
- Identify audience, intention, and intended effect.
- Explore more than one concept or narrative direction.
- Develop a storyboard or equivalent plan.
- Produce and revise the work using feedback.
- Explain aesthetic and communication choices, sources, and the role of any agent-generated material.

**Evidence:** brief, concept alternatives, storyboard, draft and final artifact, revision notes, source/asset credits, and creator reflection.

**Discipline-specific quality:** intentional form, coherence, audience fit, originality, craft, ethical sourcing, and meaningful revision.

**Integrity/accessibility:** disclose AI-generated or AI-assisted assets; verify factual claims in explanatory works; provide captions/transcript or an equivalent accessible version where relevant; respect licenses and consent.

## 4. Shared learner protocol

For each studio:

1. **Before agent use:** learner records the question, current understanding, prediction or initial design, and uncertainties.
2. **Bounded assistance:** learner specifies whether the agent may explain, ask questions, find candidate sources, critique, generate alternatives, or produce draft material. High-impact decisions remain with the learner/facilitator.
3. **Traceability:** record meaningful prompts, outputs, tools, source links, and what the learner accepted, changed, rejected, or could not verify.
4. **Independent check:** learner explains a key mechanism, justifies a source interpretation, reproduces a calculation, or defends a design/creative decision without relying on the agent's answer.
5. **Reflection:** learner identifies changed understanding, remaining gaps, and next steps.

Do not require collection of private conversation histories when a concise learner-authored contribution log is sufficient.

## 5. Common assessment, discipline-specific evidence

Assess each learner in five modes:
- **Explain:** can the learner explain the relevant concepts and assumptions?
- **Perform:** can the learner carry out the core disciplinary practice?
- **Verify:** can the learner inspect evidence, detect limitations, and correct errors?
- **Transfer:** can the learner adapt a method or idea to a changed context?
- **Create and account:** can the learner produce something purposeful and explain its consequences?

Use the capability progression rubric as descriptive guidance, not as a single aggregate score. Record discipline-specific criteria separately. A polished deliverable does not substitute for evidence of understanding.

## 6. Transfer probe

After the studio task, give learners a short unfamiliar scenario that changes at least one important condition. Ask them to:
- identify which concepts or methods remain useful;
- identify what assumptions no longer hold;
- choose a revised method;
- explain what new evidence is needed.

The transfer probe must be authored and reviewed by someone other than the primary content author where feasible. It should test adaptation, not trick knowledge.

## 7. Pilot design and data minimization

This is a small formative pilot, not a controlled efficacy trial. Before recruiting:
- define age range, language, prior experience, accessibility accommodations, and consent process;
- obtain appropriate institutional/guardian approval where required;
- recruit a diverse but manageable group and report the actual sample size;
- use the same broad assessment modes while preserving discipline-specific criteria;
- collect only data necessary to evaluate learning and usability;
- anonymize notes and obtain permission before retaining or publishing learner artifacts.

Record baseline and post-task evidence, task conditions, agent assistance level, learner reflections, reviewer observations, accessibility barriers, and revisions. Do not infer causal effectiveness from a small uncontrolled pilot.

## 8. Analysis plan

For each studio, report:
- which learning-cycle stages learners actually used;
- quality of explanations and artifacts against predeclared criteria;
- evidence of independent verification;
- performance on the transfer probe;
- where agent assistance helped, distracted, or introduced error;
- time, resource, and accessibility barriers;
- disagreement between reviewers and unresolved interpretation;
- changes required to the methodology or tutorial.

Compare patterns across studios cautiously. Differences may arise from task difficulty, learner background, facilitation, or disciplinary standards rather than the learning method itself.

## 9. Stop and revision rules

Pause a studio if the task is unsafe, inaccessible without reasonable alternative, source rights are unclear, or learners are pressured to disclose private information. Do not publish learner work without permission.

Revise the methodology if the same ambiguity or failure appears in more than one studio. Revise a discipline-specific module when its evidence standards are inappropriate for that field. Record negative and null observations; do not select only successful examples.

## 10. Readiness gates

- [ ] Each studio has an identified qualified content reviewer.
- [ ] Sources, materials, licenses, and safety/accessibility arrangements are checked.
- [ ] Tasks and assessment criteria are finalized before learner participation.
- [ ] Consent, privacy, and retention procedures are approved.
- [ ] Facilitators understand the agent-assistance protocol.
- [ ] Transfer probes are independently reviewed.
- [ ] Pilot observations are analyzed and limitations reported.

No learner pilot or expert approval is claimed complete by publication of this design brief.
